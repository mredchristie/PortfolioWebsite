Secure Package Registry (SPR)

Final Year Project, Cardiff University

Most package security tools tell you about a bad dependency after you have already installed it. SPR flips that around: every version gets run in a sandbox and checked for tampering before it reaches anyone. My final year project at Cardiff.

▸ Every package version runs in a sandbox on GitHub Actions runners while eBPF tracing watches what it actually does: files touched, network calls made, DNS looked up
▸ Rebuilds each package from source and compares the result, which catches a release that does not match the code it claims to come from
▸ Event-driven services talking over RabbitMQ, so a slow scan never blocks the registry
▸ Every version comes out with a risk level, and anything that fails is kept out of the registry entirely, so it never reaches an npm install. That closes the window Snyk and npm audit leave open

Portalguard

Public wifi blocks everything until you accept the terms on the login page. A full tunnel VPN can't get through that block. So you turn the VPN off, log in, then turn it back on. Your traffic is exposed for that whole window. PortalGuard closes it. It locks the connection down, lets only the portal login through, then hands control back to the VPN.

UK Weather Consensus

Most forecast apps average the models together. This one scores them instead. It remembers what six models predicted, checks each against what actually happened at your nearest station, and then follows whichever has been most accurate for that measurement in that place.

▸ Pulls UKMO, ECMWF, GFS, ICON, Meteo-France and MET Norway, and stores every hourly prediction for 30 days
▸ Scores each model against real station readings, per measurement and per location, so it might trust one model for rain and another for wind
▸ Confidence is based on how wrong that model has recently been, rather than being decorative
▸ Click any hour to see what each model said, including where they disagree

UK Surf Forecast

Raw marine data does not tell you much on its own. A 2m swell is brilliant at one beach and useless at the next depending on which way it faces. This works that out for 15 UK breaks and gives each one a star rating.

▸ Swell height, period and direction from Open-Meteo, plus sea state, water temperature and land wind
▸ Works out whether the wind is offshore, cross-shore or onshore for each break using the direction the beach faces
▸ Turns all of it into a 1 to 5 star rating, because that is the bit you actually want

Cornish Water Watch

South West Water publish which storm overflows are discharging right now, but not what happened yesterday. There is no history to look up, so I started keeping one. A job runs every 15 minutes and records what it finds, alongside six years of audited Environment Agency figures, and the whole thing is served as an open API.

▸ A Cloudflare Worker polls every 15 minutes into D1 and logs its own health, so a dead job cannot be mistaken for a quiet week with no spills
▸ Backfilled 2020 to 2025 from Environment Agency spreadsheets, where the columns, ID scheme and even the units change between years
▸ A spill can end in three states, not two. The third is for when a monitor drops offline mid-discharge and nobody actually knows when it stopped
▸ Every response says where the number came from and whether it has been audited, because the live feed has not been
TypeScript Cloudflare Workers D1 / SQLite OpenAPI
